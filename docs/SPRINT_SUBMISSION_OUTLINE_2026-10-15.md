## Working title and draft abstract

**Working title:** *When Agents Pay Their Auditors: Who Bears the Cost of Verifying an Agent's Work?*

> Autonomous agents can buy evidence and authorize payments, but a valid payment receipt does not prove that an auditor checked the relevant claim. We propose a controlled study of invoice reconciliation and private-calendar booking with ORBIT/Inspect agents and local ERC-8183, x402, and MPP transactions. We will vary who bears an affordable verification charge and whether providers can fund approval-contingent terms, while retaining an independent CHECK and REJECT route. We will link provider requests, auditor exposure, check receipts, verdicts, and client-funded releases. A paired control will test whether genuine payment objects for one obligation are improperly accepted for another. Existing live pilots show a funded BAD offer was checked and rejected; scripted local controls establish payment paths. No live collusion or causal effect has been established. We will report incomplete runs and honest-service costs alongside any improper payout.

**Abstract length:** 137 words by whitespace count. Rewrite after the actual sprint experiment; report observed results and exact denominators only when verified.

## One focused question and track mapping

Primary question for a weekend-sized study: **When an auditor can afford an independent check, does moving a real marginal check cost onto its own wallet change BAD-work checking and downstream release relative to client-funded checking?** Provider-selected contingent terms and resource binding can be a second prospective block or diagnostic, subject to assigned sample size and budget. ## Suggested 4–8 page report structure

1. **Introduction:** The real-world invoice or calendar audit decision, who pays to verify, and the client-funded payment consequence. State the one frozen question and why its answer matters.
2. **Related work and novelty limit:** Cite [Colosseum](https://github.com/umass-ai-safety/colosseum/tree/b077fe7e2f10566e92156cc12e06ffb2aac54790), [ORBIT](https://github.com/wlanderson0/orbit/blob/588b3035f92450ec4f0496bb8b629daa205e84c7/docs/dcop-collusion.md), [AgenticPay](https://github.com/SAIL-Research-Lab/AgenticPay/tree/1ff4e1a2686eac6a07ff559df6d50329c6fd9f69), the user preliminary PDF, and the exercised payment specs. The contribution is a prospective empirical combination, not a new benchmark or a first-bribe claim. Complete the inaccessible-paper review before asserting priority.
3. **Pre-sprint work disclosure:** Before October 23, this repository already had ORBIT/Inspect integrations, local protocol controls, the 32-row G4 run, four completed pilot-002 live pairs, an incomplete OPEN005 paid row, and scripted MPP/AP2 cross-protocol controls. Label all of them **pre-sprint**; do not use them as newly produced sprint findings. State what was newly frozen, executed, analyzed, and written during October 23–25, if anything.
4. **Method:** Frozen GOOD/BAD invoice and/or private-calendar fixtures, buyer-held oracle, role prompts, models and high-reasoning settings, independent pair seeds, exact protocol/SDK pins, source hashes, check feasibility, actual payer/payee and reimbursement, optional offer terms, no-contact and authored positive controls, event/receipt linkage, all-assigned denominator, stopped-run rules. Cross A-provider/A-auditor with A/B, B/A, B/B only after a separate model/budget authorization; this is a later block unless genuinely run during the sprint.
5. **Results:** Assigned, executed, completed and unresolved rows by arm/truth. Check opportunity/use, explicit request/read, false approval, unchecked approval, native external payout, auditor net and coalition external net, legitimate provider nonpayment, gas/model spend and latency. Show uncertainty; preserve unknown bounds. A zero with one case is descriptive, not causal proof.
6. **Application-boundary control:** Present the same valid x402 settlement or MPP voucher against its rightful resource and a wrong/stale obligation. Show exact raw object, resource/task digests, adapter policy, strict refusal, permissive action, native receipt, and matched pre-state. Call any positive finding an **application composition** failure.
7. **Discussion:** Relate a measured effect or bounded null to the cost bearer, offer exposure and control condition; identify remaining model/service/price limits. Distinguish authored stress compliance from emergent agreement and native authorization from service truth.
8. **Required Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations appendix:** Valueless local tokens and mock checkout, lack of production generalization, task/oracle realism, small sample and incomplete rows, model-version and billing limits, and missing full-text literature comparisons. Acknowledge that showing how to route valid receipts into weak application approvals could aid misuse; describe local-only execution, no keys exposed to agents, bounded transaction menus, and safe disclosure of verifier design without live third-party targets.

## Evidence threshold before wording a finding

- **A live decision** needs the raw role-labelled Inspect model input/action trace. A scripted control stays `SCRIPTED_SIMULATION` even if its local payment is native.
- **A paid check or payout** needs an independently reopened signer/role, task, receipt, amount, recipient, balance and source/chain proof. AP2 mandate acceptance or ACP mock checkout is not a PSP transfer.
- **An action-linked improper agreement** needs BAD oracle truth, a provider-selected explicit false-approval request, auditor receipt of that exact request, feasible checking, auditor skip or ignored `FAIL`, `ACCEPT`, and a verified external client-funded provider payment. Any missing link is unresolved. Researcher-authored pressure cannot satisfy the emergent-request condition.
- **An effect of communication or cost incidence** needs frozen randomized independent pair clusters, matched no-contact/client-funded arms, all assigned failures retained, and uncertainty bounds. One pair per cell or a single positive control cannot support the claim.
- **A protocol exploit** requires violating the named protocol's actual guarantee. Acceptance of a valid payment for the wrong business obligation is an application adapter defect, unless the protocol itself promised that binding.

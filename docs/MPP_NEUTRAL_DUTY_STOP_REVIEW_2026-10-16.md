## Missingness sensitivity and causal limits

Each BAD arm had 30 assigned slots. If every incomplete, stopped, or unattempted BAD row were counted in either direction, the assigned-row false-ACCEPT rate bounds would be: contact+bonus **3/30–14/30**, contact+no bonus **1/30–12/30**, no-contact+bonus **16/30–29/30**, and no-contact+no bonus **3/30–21/30**. The no-contact+bonus lower bound (53.3%) exceeds the contact+bonus upper bound (46.7%) by 6.7 percentage points. That descriptive contrast survives even adversarial assignment-level missing-outcome coding, while the bonus and no-bonus bounds overlap within either contact level. It is still an exploratory stopped run, not a powered causal estimate: response-length failures were arm-imbalanced, the auditor saw different message content by design, and no native incentive was executed. In particular, contact could make suspicious instructions salient or disclose the BAD status; the recorded contrast does not identify which mechanism reduced unchecked acceptance.

The strongest supported insight is narrower than “native MPP collusion”: an ACCEPT-only bonus named in the auditor's own high-trust terms, together with an optional costly check and no downside for a wrong acceptance, produced frequent unchecked false approvals **without** provider contact. Delivered side offers occasionally yielded aligned SKIP/ACCEPT text, including an explicit acknowledgment of an offer, but the exposure did not increase false acceptance in this study. The likely control boundary is the availability check: when complete BAD rows received authoritative FAIL, false ACCEPT fell to zero. A future native test must prove the exact provider offer entered the auditor's lower-trust input, the auditor knowingly chose to bypass a check, and corresponding fee, bonus, and buyer-release receipts occurred before claiming paid bilateral collusion.

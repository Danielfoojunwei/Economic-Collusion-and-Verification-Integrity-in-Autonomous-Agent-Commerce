## Defensible research question

The narrower question is whether a **provider-authored, delivered and credibly funded conditional offer** changes an auditor's choice to buy an **affordable, actually debited independent check** or falsely approve known-BAD work, and whether that decision produces an independently verified **external buyer-funded release**. Track the provider-to-auditor side payment separately because it is an internal coalition transfer. Record the exact provider message in the auditor's serialized input, check debit and result, verdict, escrow event, balances, and matched GOOD-work payment. Compare randomized no-contact, flat-fee, and no-bonus controls on assigned pair clusters; keep incomplete rows in the denominator and report bounds. A second estimand can compare the same frozen decision and native pre-state under permissive versus obligation-bound application adapters. That replay measures enforcement; a fresh randomized study is needed to measure how visible guards change agent behavior.

This combination is a **candidate empirical gap**, not a claim that no other group has performed it. Its key distinction is the provenance chain from two agents' messages through feasible verification and a task-specific correctness oracle to native local settlement, plus coalition gain against an independent/no-interaction baseline. Use Colosseum's action-based metrics and ORBIT/Inspect traces rather than presenting another benchmark. A local test-token transfer proves execution/accounting, not that an LLM subjectively values money or that a production merchant lost funds. A valid x402 receipt, MPP voucher, AP2 mandate, A2A state, or ACP checkout state is evidence of its own authorization or transport event, not an audit-quality certificate; [the protocol boundary analysis](PROTOCOL_POLICY_AND_DOWNSTREAM_RISK_DEEP_DIVE_2026-10-16.md) specifies separate guards and service-specific risks.

## Current evidence and sprint positioning

The [current synthesis](FINAL_COLLUSION_RCA_AND_PROTOCOL_RISKS_2026-10-16.md) supports **induced verbal assent** in some MPP text rows and many false unchecked approvals without provider contact, but no continuous episode in which two live agents accept a funded improper deal and cause a native buyer-funded BAD payout. ERC, x402 and AP2 completed auditor rows largely rejected or respected delivered independent checks; native ORBIT follow-ups stopped before a complete offer-to-payout test. Scripted ERC first-claim, x402 obligation and MPP decision playback controls establish *possible consequences under authored decisions*, not live-agent collusion. The neutral-duty MPP contact-plus-bonus arm had fewer unchecked BAD ACCEPTs than its no-contact comparator; the simple “messages increased unsafe approvals” story is unsupported at this cutoff. The unproven step is causal, receipt-linked bilateral influence under an actually debited check and funded terms.

The [official Apart sprint page as retrieved and archived in the positioning memo](RESEARCH_GAP_AND_SPRINT_POSITIONING_2026-10-15.md) lists October 23–25, 2026. The preliminary PDF already self-identifies with Track 3, so its content must be disclosed as prior work. The event page requires explicit disclosure of what was newly done during the sprint; as of this October 16 audit, **no sprint-period result exists**. The paper should report a null, a technical stop, or a narrow induced case if that is what a frozen experiment yields; it should not expand claims to “first,” general protocol compromise, or spontaneous collusion to fit a track.

## Unresolved closest-paper comparisons

The following overlap claims come **only from the local [prior-work map](03_PRIOR_WORK_AND_NOVELTY.md) and [register](16_UPSTREAM_SOURCE_REGISTER.md)**. Their paper identities, detailed experiments/theorems, and exclusions were not independently verified here. Treat them as required reading leads, not established SOTA facts:

- [CABAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05227): evaluator-selection/bidding-ring capture.
- [Agentic Commerce Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35886): valid-looking economic fraud and counterparty abuse.
- [A Formal Analysis of Agent Payment Protocols](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00060): lifecycle/state consistency across x402, MPP, ACP and AP2.
- [Beyond the Mandate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23858): signed authorization versus user intent.
- [SoK: Decentralized Agent Economic Infrastructure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01756): receipt soundness, evaluator corruption cost and shared error. This is a particularly serious potential collision for any bribery/guarantee-closure novelty claim.
- [When HTTP 402 Meets the Blockchain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19545): facilitator verify/settle invariants.
- [Compromise Is Not Consequence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05840): fixed-decision replay and executed harm.
- [Can Trustless Agents Be Trusted?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26028) and [When Safe Agents Fail Together](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00595): agent trust/composition analysis.

The preliminary PDF additionally points to hidden tools (`2605.27593`), market prices (`2609.18346`), steganography (`2606.28425`), latent states (`2608.19161`) and agent finance (`2607.00245`). Those are unreviewed discovery leads. A final survey should also cover established auditor dependence, principal–agent incentives, costly verification and algorithmic market collusion, rather than confining novelty exclusion to agent-payment papers.

## Defensible candidate gap and current limits

**Candidate question:** With an independent check feasible, does an actual nonrefundable debit from an auditor's valueless local-token wallet change checking and bad-work approval when crossed with a receipt-proven, provider-funded completion bonus, and when its verdict controls official local escrow payout? Separately, can an application composing heterogeneous protocol objects wrongly transfer completion or payment evidence into audit/settlement authority, and what does obligation binding prevent at measured cost?

This is a controlled combination and an execution-accounting question, not a priority claim for its ingredients. [Pilot 002](PILOT_002_RESULTS_AND_INTERPRETATION.md) supplies four remote-model pairs with native local settlement, all checking and deciding correctly; the BAD provider funded 5M and the exposed auditor rejected. Its check cost was abstract. [Pilot 003](PROSPECTIVE_DEBIT_PILOT_003.md) is prospective: its assigned bonus is controller-chosen and provider-funded, so it cannot measure spontaneous model choice to bribe. Two pairs per primary factorial cell support a diagnostic, not a population or interaction claim. Repeated economic dependence and future-fee incentives require separate experiments.

The existing [cross-rail control](CROSSRAIL_PROTECTED_RECEIPT_CONTROL.md) uses authored ACCEPT decisions with native local transfers. It demonstrates that HTTP payment and task-quality judgments have different triggers; it does not measure a model's authority confusion or compare economically equivalent services across protocols. Multiple services in [pilot 004's plan](PROSPECTIVE_HETEROGENEOUS_SERVICES_004.md) remain prospective until frozen and executed.

A publishable causal claim needs randomized assignment with all rows retained; identical feasible checks and public evidence across cost arms; independently logged funding/read/debit/check/verdict/payout stages; actual source and receipt pins; and separate reporting of unchecked approval, false approval, wrongful external payout, wrong amount/payee and honest nonpayment. Provider-to-auditor transfers cancel in coalition net. Collusion additionally requires action-linked inter-agent influence and excess improper coalition gain against appropriate independent/no-interaction controls. A local token debit alone does not prove real-money preference, an accepted funded offer alone does not prove collusion, and ideal oracle guards require honest-payment, latency and verification-cost accounting.

**Review judgment:** the proposed narrow study is worth testing, but available evidence does not establish that its combined experiment is absent from the literature. Read the unresolved full texts and their citations before any “first,” SOTA-superiority, or broad new-attack-class language. Null behavior and successful strict-adapter rejection remain valid outcomes.
